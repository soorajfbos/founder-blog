## Why the Best MVP Process Depends on Scope, Budget, and Certainty

There is no single development process that works perfectly for every startup MVP. The right approach depends on how clearly the product is defined, how much the startup can spend, and how likely the requirements are to change.

### Start With the Product Scope

A clearly defined MVP is easier to plan and manage.

Founders should identify the essential features and avoid adding unnecessary functionality before development begins.

### Consider the Budget

Startups need to make careful use of their available resources.

For startups using [mvp development services for startups](https://foundersbar.com/articles-and-research/why-waterfall-is-better-than-agile-for-startup-mvp-development), a controlled scope can make it easier to estimate development effort and protect the planned budget.

### Evaluate Product Certainty

Founders should consider how much they already know about the product.

If customer needs, user flows, and core features are well understood, a structured approach may be suitable.

If the product is still being tested, a flexible approach may provide more room for learning and changes.

### Think About Expected Changes

Frequent requirement changes can affect development time and cost.

If the startup expects the MVP requirements to remain stable, planning the project in defined stages can provide greater predictability.

### Consider the Launch Timeline

Some startups need to reach the market by a specific date.

A clear development sequence can help the team organize requirements, design, development, testing, and launch preparation around that target.

### Look at Technical Complexity

The technology involved can also influence the best development process.

Products with complex integrations or technical dependencies may benefit from detailed planning before implementation begins.

### Balance Structure and Flexibility

A structured process can provide clear milestones and predictable execution.

A flexible process can make it easier to respond to customer feedback and changing requirements.

Founders should choose the balance that matches their product rather than assuming one method is always better.

### Avoid Unnecessary Features

The MVP should focus on its primary purpose.

Features that are not required for the first launch can be placed on a future roadmap. This helps keep development focused and prevents unnecessary increases in scope.

### Review the Methodology Before Development

The development methodology should be selected before major implementation begins.

Founders can review their scope, budget, timeline, technical requirements, and level of uncertainty before making the decision.

### Build According to the Project

The best MVP process is the one that fits the startup's actual situation.

A well-defined product with stable requirements may benefit from a structured approach, while an uncertain product may need more flexibility.

By considering scope, budget, timeline, and product certainty together, founders can choose a development process that supports a focused and practical path from idea to launch.
