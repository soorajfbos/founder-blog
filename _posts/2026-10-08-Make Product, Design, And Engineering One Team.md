## Make Product, Design, And Engineering One Team

Great teams move as one. They share a goal, talk often, and ship small slices. You can set this up with a few simple habits. It will raise speed and cut rework.

### Pick One North Star

Choose one main metric for the next two cycles. Add three support metrics. For example:
- North star: weekly active teams
- Support: activation by day 7, task success rate, NPS

Share this in every planning doc. If a task does not help a metric, drop it.

### Run A Weekly PDE Sync

Hold a 30-minute product, design, and engineering sync:
- Review what shipped and what users did
- Decide the next slice to ship
- Remove blockers and reset dates if needed

Keep notes in one doc with owners and dates. Decisions should be easy to find.

### Use A One Page PRD

Write a short plan for each bet:
- Problem and users
- Outcome and metric
- Constraints and risks
- Test plan and rollout

Skip long specs. A clear one-pager guides the team without slowing them down.

### Move From Talk To Tests

Prototype or ship a small slice in a week. Put it behind a flag. Try it with 5 to 10 users. Watch what they do. Use that to shape the next slice. Repeat. This loop builds the right thing faster.

### Show The Work

Do a biweekly demo. Show live screens. Share wins and misses. End with one ask, one risk, and one change for next time. People rally around real progress.

### The Founder’s Role

- Pick the few bets that matter
- Say no to work that does not help the metrics
- Clear blockers fast
- Protect focus time

If you need a short boost to set this up before a full-time hire, explore [fractional cto for startups](https://foundersbar.com/articles-and-research/when-to-hire-a-fractional-cto-vs-full-time-cto). A part-time leader can set the system and hand it back to your team.
