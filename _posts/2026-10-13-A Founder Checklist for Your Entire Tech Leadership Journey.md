## A Founder Checklist for Your Entire Tech Leadership Journey

Technical leadership is not one decision. It is a series of choices across your company's life. This checklist pulls the key moments together so you can see the whole path and prepare for the next step.

### Before You Have a Product

Do this now:
- Write down the technical problem you are solving in one paragraph
- Find one technical advisor you can call each month
- Learn the basics: what a stack is, what uptime means, what cloud costs
- Decide whether technology or market is your biggest risk

Skip this:
- Hiring a full-time technical executive
- Giving away co-founder equity after one meeting
- Choosing a complex architecture

### Building Version One

Do this now:
- Hire one or two strong builders, or use a vetted agency with clear ownership terms
- Make sure your company owns all code, accounts, and domains
- Set up basic code review and a simple deploy process
- Write a one-page system map
- Keep a decision log

Skip this:
- Microservices
- A large team
- Long technical specs

### After You Have Real Users

Do this now:
- Track uptime, incidents, and cycle time
- Set up backups and test a restore
- Turn on MFA everywhere
- Write a one-page data map
- Start a technical debt list

Watch for these signals:
- Delivery slipping repeatedly
- You becoming the bottleneck for every technical call
- Customers asking for security proof

### Choosing Your Leadership Path

Ask these questions:
- What must be achieved in the next 12 months?
- Will that take more than 20 hours a week of senior time?
- Are we hiring three or more engineers this quarter?
- Can our runway support a full-time executive?

Then choose: a senior builder, part-time guidance, or a full-time leader. For the full framework on this choice and when to hire a cto, see [hire a cto](https://foundersbar.com/articles-and-research/when-to-hire-a-fractional-cto-vs-full-time-cto).

### Running the Search

Do this now:
- Write a two-page scorecard with measurable outcomes
- Set your cash and equity range before the first call
- Keep the interview loop to four sessions in one week
- Include a real working session with your team
- Call three references, including one former direct report
- Decide within 48 hours of the final session

Skip this:
- Generic job boards
- More than five must-have requirements
- Skipping references because you are in a hurry

### The First 90 Days

Do this now:
- Share a written 90-day plan before day one
- Agree on who owns what in a one-page table
- Set spending limits in writing
- Hold a weekly 30-minute one on one
- Ask for one visible win by day 30
- Run a full review at day 90

### Ongoing Management

Every month:
- Review delivery, uptime, and cost numbers
- Give specific feedback, good and bad

Every quarter:
- Review outcomes against the agreed plan
- Ask what they need from you
- Check whether the structure still fits the team size

Every year:
- Revisit comp and equity
- Plan for the next stage of growth
- Ask directly whether they want the job the next stage requires

### Warning Signs to Never Ignore

- Three cycles in a row of missed dates with no clear explanation
- Repeated incidents with no postmortem
- Engineers leaving and citing leadership in exit conversations
- Your leader going quiet or stopping pushback
- Cloud costs rising faster than users

Address these within two weeks of noticing them.

### The Three Rules That Matter Most

1. Hire for the work in front of you, not the company you hope to be in three years.
2. Write everything down: scope, outcomes, decisions, and ownership.
3. Give real authority or do not hire at all.

### Final Word

Your technical leadership choice is one of the biggest calls you will make. Take it seriously, but do not freeze. Start with the smallest option that solves your next milestone, measure honestly, and grow from there. Clarity beats perfection every time.
