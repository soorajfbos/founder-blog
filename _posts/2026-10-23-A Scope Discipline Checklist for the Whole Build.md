## A Scope Discipline Checklist for the Whole Build

This brings together the practical checks that keep a build on budget. Twenty minutes with this list at each stage prevents most of the expensive mistakes.

### Before You Design Anything

- Write your budget ceiling, your reserve, and your week count
- Interview five target users and watch how they work today
- Write the one number that proves the product works
- List three must-have features
- Write ten things you are not building, with reasons for each
- Decide the maximum spend before you must see real usage

The exclusion list is the single most useful document in the project.

### Before You Commit to a Supplier

Confirm every one of these:
- Phased contract with no more than 30 percent upfront
- Acceptance tests written in plain words for each feature
- A clickable demo every Friday, written into the agreement
- Code in your repository from day one with daily commits
- All accounts in your company name with your billing
- IP transfers on creation, not on payment
- Handover deliverables listed as a condition of final payment
- Either side can stop at a phase boundary

Reluctance on any of these is your clearest warning signal.

### Before Building Starts

- Build and test a clickable prototype with five users
- Spike the riskiest assumption for two days
- Sketch the data model on paper
- Agree the change process in writing
- Set a visible contingency, around 15 percent
- Put the midpoint checkpoint date in the calendar

### Every Week During the Build

- Click through something new on Friday
- Update three numbers: features done, weeks used, budget spent
- Spend five minutes on changes requested, approved, and contingency left
- Send or receive one paragraph: shipped, next, blocked, risk

### At the Midpoint

If less than 60 percent of planned work is done, cut scope that day. Do not add money. Do not add people. Do not hope for a strong finish.

### Warning Signs That Need Action

Two or more of these means act this week:
- The demo shows the same screen as last week
- Estimates moving by a few days repeatedly
- Work appearing that is not in the plan
- Contingency draining faster than weeks elapsed
- Answers getting vaguer

### Before You Accept Delivery

- Every acceptance test passes
- Three target users complete the main task unaided
- Empty states and error states look intentional
- Real data loads without breaking layouts
- No customer can see another customer's records
- Setup instructions work on a clean machine
- You can deploy and roll back yourself

### At Launch

- Ten users already committed to trying it
- 20 percent of budget held in reserve
- Budget alerts set on cloud and tools
- Backups running with a tested restore
- Error tracking and uptime alerts live

### The Five Mistakes That Cost Most

1. No written exclusion list, so scope grows unnoticed
2. Spending the entire budget on the build with nothing held back
3. Waiting more than two weeks to see working software
4. Not owning the code and accounts from day one
5. Continuing past the point where the evidence said stop

### The Three Habits That Matter Most

- Write it down before you build it
- See something you can click every single week
- Decide in advance what would make you stop

Everything you agree with a [mvp development service](https://foundersbar.com/articles-and-research/how-to-build-an-mvp-without-going-over-budget) should connect back to these three.

### Start Where You Are

If you are mid-build and missing half this list, do not panic. Pick the three items with the highest risk and fix them this week. A project with weekly demos, clear ownership, and a written exclusion list is in far better shape than a tidy plan with none of them.
