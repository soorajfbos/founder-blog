## The Founder's Checklist: Before You Hire Your MVP Development Team

### Before you start building
Use this checklist to make sure you are ready:

### Product clarity
- [ ] Can you explain your product in one sentence?
- [ ] Do you know your target user (one specific person)?
- [ ] Do you know their biggest pain point?
- [ ] Do you have evidence that they want this solved? (Talked to 10+ people?)
- [ ] Have you defined your core metric (how will you know this works)?

### Scope clarity
- [ ] Can you list 5-7 steps of the user journey?
- [ ] Have you defined must-haves (what ships day one)?
- [ ] Have you defined won't-haves (what waits for phase two)?
- [ ] Have you written acceptance rules for each feature? (How will you test it?)
- [ ] Have you locked scope and are ready to say no to extras?

### Business clarity
- [ ] Do you have runway for 3-4 months?
- [ ] Have you set a budget for the MVP? (With 20% buffer?)
- [ ] Do you know how you will measure success after launch?
- [ ] Have you thought about how you will reach users after launch?
- [ ] Do you know who your first paying customers might be?

### Team clarity
- [ ] Have you decided: Will you hire, contract, or co-found?
- [ ] If contracting, do you have 2-3 options and have you interviewed them?
- [ ] Do you know what skills you need (frontend, backend, design, etc.)?
- [ ] Have you checked references on at least one candidate?
- [ ] Do you have a backup plan if the first team does not work out?

### Process clarity
- [ ] Have you decided on a tech stack? (And why?)
- [ ] Have you set a timeline (e.g., ship in 8 weeks)?
- [ ] Have you decided on a communication cadence? (Weekly demos? Daily standups?)
- [ ] Have you picked a product owner who will make final calls?
- [ ] Have you written a simple one-pager for the spec?

### Preparation
- [ ] Have you set up a shared code repo (GitHub)?
- [ ] Have you set up project management (Trello, Asana)?
- [ ] Have you set up communication (Slack)?
- [ ] Have you created a simple contract or statement of work?
- [ ] Have you briefed your team and advisors on the plan?

### If you checked all boxes
You are ready to hire a developer or team.

Start with a short conversation:
- Share your spec and timeline.
- Ask them: Can you do this?
- Ask them: What could go wrong?
- Ask them: What would you do differently?

If they ask smart questions and you trust their answers, you have found the right partner.

### If you could not check all boxes
Do not hire yet.

- If you are unclear on product: Talk to 10 more users. Get clarity.
- If scope is fuzzy: Sit down and write the spec. Lock it.
- If you do not have budget: Fundraise or reduce scope.
- If you do not have runway: Get a co-founder or investor.
- If you have no process: Design it before you start.

Building without clarity wastes time and money. Spend a week getting clear. Then build fast.

### Final thought
The founder's job is to set up the team for success. That means clarity on what you are building, who you are building for, how much time and money you have, and who will lead. Get those five things right and a good team will execute. Get them wrong and no team can save you. Do the work upfront. Then let them build.

**Appendix: Quick reference for hiring your MVP development team**

When you interview a potential team, ask these questions:

1. Show me an MVP you shipped in 8-12 weeks. Can I talk to the founder?
2. How do you handle scope creep? What is your change process?
3. What is your weekly cadence? How do I see progress?
4. If we hit a blocker, how do you solve it?
5. What would you cut to ship on time and why?
6. How do you write and test code?
7. What is the tech stack and why?
8. What is your rate and how is it structured? (Milestone-based?)
9. What happens if we want to end the contract early?
10. What do you need from us to succeed?

A team worth hiring will have clear, thoughtful answers to all ten questions. A team that hedges or gives vague answers is not ready for an MVP.
