## A Founder Checklist for Every Stage of Your First Build

Building a first product is a long sequence of small decisions. This checklist pulls the key ones together so you can see the whole path and prepare for what comes next.

### Before You Spend Anything

Do this:
- Interview five target users and watch how they work today
- Write one sentence naming a narrow user group
- Write the single number that proves the product works
- List three must-have features and five things you are not building
- Decide the maximum you will spend before seeing real usage

Skip this:
- Choosing a tech stack
- Hiring a team
- Designing screens

### During Planning and Discovery

Do this:
- Run a two week discovery with a clear deliverable
- Build a clickable prototype and test with five users
- Sketch the data model on paper
- Decide the account boundary: individuals or teams
- Test your riskiest assumption directly

Deliverable: a one page plan anyone can read in two minutes.

### Before You Sign With a Builder

Confirm:
- The contract is phased, with no more than 30 percent upfront
- Deliverables are listed in plain words
- Acceptance tests define done for each feature
- Your company owns the code from the moment it is written
- All accounts are in your name with your billing
- You will see a clickable demo every Friday
- Either side can stop at a phase boundary

If any answer is unclear, do not sign yet.

### During the Build

Every week:
- Click through something new on Friday
- Update three numbers: features done, days used, open bugs
- Send a one paragraph note on shipped, next, blocked, risk

At the midpoint:
- If less than 60 percent of work is done, cut scope that day
- Review the not building list and hold the line

Warning signs to act on:
- "Almost done" for two weeks running
- Demos showing the same screen as last week
- Work happening on things not in the plan

### Before You Accept Delivery

Check:
- Every acceptance test passes
- Three target users complete the main task without help
- Empty states and error states look intentional
- Real data loads without breaking layouts
- No customer can see another customer's records
- Setup instructions work on a clean machine
- You can deploy and roll back yourself

### At Launch

Have ready:
- Ten users already committed to trying it
- A personal onboarding call booked with each
- Error tracking and uptime alerts running
- A tested backup and restore
- Budget alerts on your cloud and tools
- Twenty percent of your budget held back for fixes

### In the First Thirty Days

Each week:
- Call two users
- Fix the biggest drop in your funnel
- Ship something visible

At day thirty, write one page: what happened, what you learned, what you will focus on next.

### Stop Rules to Write in Advance

- Week four: a real user completes the main task in a working version
- Week eight: three of five target users finish without help
- Week twelve: your main number moved or three users would pay

Decide now what pass, adjust, and stop each mean.

### The Five Mistakes That Cost the Most

1. Building for months without a user clicking anything
2. No written definition of done
3. Code and accounts owned by someone else
4. Scope growing quietly with no list of what you are skipping
5. Continuing long after the evidence said stop

### The Three Rules That Matter Most

- Write it down before you build it
- See working software every single week
- Decide in advance what would make you stop

Everything else in [custom mvp development](https://foundersbar.com/articles-and-research/startup-technical-blueprint) is detail compared to these three habits.

### Start Where You Are

If you are mid-build and missing half this list, do not panic. Pick the three items with the biggest risk and fix them this week. A messy project with weekly demos and clear ownership is in far better shape than a tidy plan with neither.
